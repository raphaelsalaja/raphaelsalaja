The software I keep using is quiet. It doesn't celebrate when I finish a task or nudge me back, and it shows up, does the thing and gets out of the way.

I didn't notice this for a long time. I'd try a new tool, like it for a week and drift back to the old one without being able to say why, until eventually I saw the pattern: the ones I left were all a little loud.

## What Noise Looks Like

A lot of products are built to be noticed: motion on every click, a gradient that exists so a screenshot looks alive and copy that talks like someone who wants to be your friend. None of that helps me do the work.

Noise is anything that asks for attention without giving something back: a badge that counts things I don't care about, a tooltip that appears every time I hover the same button, a confirmation for an action I can undo or a modal announcing a feature in the middle of the thing I opened the app to do.

Each one is small, and each one was probably added for a good reason: someone wanted engagement on a feature, someone wanted the product to feel friendly or someone wanted to make sure nobody missed the new thing. Together they make a product that's always talking, so after a while people stop listening, including when it says something important.

<sub>In the loud version a tick shakes the row, bursts confetti out of the box, turns the task purple and springs up a cheering toast for two seconds. In the quiet version the box fills and the task dims under a line.</sub>

The loud version isn't wrong to want to reward you, because finishing something feels good and it's tempting to amplify that. But the celebration is the product's idea of how you should feel, played at you every time, so by the third task the confetti is the thing between you and the next item.

The quiet version says exactly what happened: the box is checked and the text dims, so the task is done and you move on. If you want to feel good about it you can, but the product isn't deciding that for you.

## Quiet Is a Decision

Quiet doesn't happen by default. Most products drift louder over time, because every new feature wants to be found and every team wants their work noticed, so staying quiet means saying no to a lot of that.

It shows up in small choices. This transition is short because it'll be seen a hundred times a day. This colour is here to make the one thing that matters easy to find. This screen has less on it than the one we almost shipped. This setting doesn't need a toast when it changes, because the change is already visible.

It also shows up in what doesn't get built: no streaks, no "you haven't opened the app in three days", no onboarding tour on the fifth visit and no sound unless the sound is useful.

## Quiet Isn't Silent

There's a version of this that goes too far: a product so minimal that nobody can tell whether a click did anything has gone past quiet into unclear.

Quiet software still gives feedback, just exactly as much as is needed and no more. A button responds when it's pressed and a save shows that it saved, once and small, before getting out of the way. An error is clear and stays until someone deals with it, and a sound, if there is one, is short and soft and tells me something I'd otherwise miss.

Feedback answers what someone just did, but noise is the product talking about itself.

## The Five Hundredth Minute

Loud products are good at first impressions: they demo well, they screenshot well and they feel alive in the first five minutes.

Quiet products are good at the five hundredth minute. They feel like a tool I own instead of a service that wants something from me, and I think the best compliment a tool can get is that someone forgets it's there.
