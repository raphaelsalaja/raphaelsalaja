There's a difference between sharing what I made and performing the making of it: one is a record, and the other is a show that starts to steer the work.

I share a lot of what I build, and I think it's good to, because it's how I've met most of the people I like working with and how a lot of us learn from each other. Posting is fine, but what I'm interested in is what happens to the work when the post becomes the point.

## The Slide

Posting a detail I care about is fine, because it forces me to look at it again at a different size and with fresh eyes. Half the time I'd notice something I wanted to fix before I hit send.

The slide happens when the detail gets picked because it will read well as a clip, so the interface is being designed for the timeline, not for the person who has to use it on a Tuesday.

It's gradual. First the choice is which thing to post, and then it's which thing to build next, based on what posted well. Then the animation gets a bit longer, because a longer animation shows up better in a ten second video, and then the work has a second client, which is the feed.

## What the Clip Shows

A product built to be screenshotted is easy to spot: the motion is longer than it needs to be, the empty state is a poster and the real states (the error, the loading, the third item in a list) are thin.

<StatesFigure />
<sub>Six states of the same list sit in a row, from empty to forty items, and a frame labelled The clip draws in around the one with three items.</sub>

A clip shows the best moment of the best state: three items with nice names and good avatars, a smooth transition and a dark background. It almost never shows the list with nothing in it, the list while it loads, the error when the network drops or the list with forty items and someone's name that wraps onto two lines.

Those states are where people spend most of their time, so polishing only what goes in the clip means polishing the part they'll see least.

## What the Timeline Rewards

The feed has its own taste, and it's not the same as the taste of someone using a product all day.

It rewards motion that reads at a glance, so things get slower and bigger. It rewards novelty, so the third tab switcher of the week has to do something the first two didn't. It rewards a single moment, so the work becomes a collection of moments instead of a product that holds together.

None of those are bad on their own, because a slower animation is fine on a screen that's seen once and a novel interaction is fine if it solves something. But if they come from the timeline instead of from the work, they're decoration, and people feel the difference.

## Designing to Be Shared

There's an honest version of designing for sharing, and I think it matters.

It's about what happens when someone sends a product to a friend, and whether the link opens on the thing they meant to share or on the home page. It's whether a screenshot of their own work in the tool looks good or is full of chrome and banners, and whether they can export something they're proud of.

That's designing something so it can be shared: it's for the person using it, and the sharing is theirs. It's the opposite of designing something so I can share it.

## A Test I Use

Before I built or posted something, I'd ask whether I'd build it this way if nobody but the people using it would ever see it. A yes means posting it is just a record, but a no, like an animation longer than I'd keep it or a state nobody will really hit, means I'm performing.

Then I'd watch it at real speed, once, the way someone would see it in the middle of their day. Clips are watched on a loop, but products are used once, then again and then again, so something that looks great on a loop can feel slow the second time someone is actually waiting for it.

<LoopFigure />
<sub>Across ten uses in a day, the 600 millisecond animation adds up to six seconds of waiting and the 200 millisecond one to two, so the longer one costs four more seconds.</sub>

I try to build it for the people using it and post it afterwards if I like, because if it only feels good in the clip, it isn't finished.
