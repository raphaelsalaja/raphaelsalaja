On my flight back from San Francisco, I sat near a group coming home from YC's AI Startup School. I expected to hear about what they were building.

For most of the flight they talked about valuations. Rounds, exits, what a company at their stage could be worth. I didn't hear anyone describe their product, or who it was for.

Money isn't the problem. You need it to build almost anything, and wanting to be paid well for good work is fine. The trouble starts when it's the only reason. Then the work is just what you have to do to get to the money, and every day you spend on it is a cost.

<PayoffFigure />
<sub>Build for the work and every day pays something. Build for the exit and all of it rides on one day that might not come.</sub>

It also changes what failure means. If you were building because you liked the work, you still had the work. If the exit was the point and it never comes, you have very little to show for the years.

Most of the people I admire built things because they wanted them to exist. They liked making them, and they hoped someone else would like them too. When someone did, that was the reason to make the next one.

<ThreadFigure />
<sub>Building feeds itself. Make something, someone loves it, and you make more. Banking runs in a line, and the line ends.</sub>

I still think that's the better reason to start something, and the one more likely to end with something people want.
