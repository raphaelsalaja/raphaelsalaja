A lot of what I see posted in design and engineering lately is made to be approved of. People copy the style and opinions of whoever already has the audience, and shape their work around what will get noticed instead of what they care about.

Some of that is just engagement farming. But it changes what people make. When approval is the measure, trying something unfamiliar is a risk, so fewer people try. It's part of why so many products look the same.

<DriftFigure />
<sub>When approval is the measure, everyone drifts toward whoever already has it, and the rest of the space goes unexplored.</sub>

It also turns a few people into idols. If you're just starting out, the distance between you and them looks impossible to close, and copying them looks like the only way across.

Before I make or post something, I try to ask one question: am I doing this because I care about it, or because I hope it gets noticed?

<sub>The same piece of work can start from either reason. Only one of them leads somewhere new.</sub>

I don't always like the answer. But when it's the first one, the work tends to be more interesting, and more mine.
