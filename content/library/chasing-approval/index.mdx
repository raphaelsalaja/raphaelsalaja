A lot of what I see posted in design and engineering lately is made to be approved of. People copy the style and opinions of whoever already has the audience, and shape their work around what will get noticed instead of what they care about.

Some of that is just engagement farming, but it still changes what people make: when approval is the measure, trying something unfamiliar is a risk, so fewer people try. It's part of why so many products look the same.

<DriftFigure />
<sub>Under making what you care about, fourteen dots sit spread out. Under making what gets noticed, the same fourteen drift in towards the one point that already has attention, and the rest of the field empties.</sub>

It also turns a few people into idols, so for someone just starting out, the distance to them looks impossible to close and copying them looks like the only way across.

Before I made or posted something, I'd try to ask one question: am I doing this because I care about it, or because I hope it gets noticed?

<ForkFigure />
<sub>Why make this splits into two paths. Because I care about it draws itself out to richer ideas, while because it might get noticed stays faint and ends at more posturing.</sub>

I didn't always like the answer, but when it was the first one, the work tended to be more interesting and more mine.
