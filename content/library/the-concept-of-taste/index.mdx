People call taste subjective, and in one sense it is, because people like what they like. But most of what feels like instinct was learned somewhere, from seeing enough good work and enough bad work that some choices started to look right and others started to look off.

## What People Have Said

The argument is old. David Hume thought taste was a skill, and wrote that some people judge better than others because they've practised, compared a lot of work and kept their prejudices out of it.[^1]

Kant thought a judgement of taste starts with a feeling, not a rule, but that someone calling something beautiful doesn't mean it's beautiful to them: they talk as if everyone should agree.[^2]

Bourdieu was less generous: he found that what people liked tracked closely with their class and education, and argued that taste works as a way of marking who belongs where.[^3]

All three are partly right: taste is personal, it can be learned and it's shaped by the people around you.

## What It's Made Of

A lot of what reads as taste is structure that can be named. Size, weight and space decide where the eye lands first, while colour sets a mood before anything is read and a typeface sets the tone of a sentence before its words have been read.

When something feels just right it usually isn't a mystery, because someone made a few of those decisions well and kept them consistent.

## It Moves

Taste changes: Baroque gave way to modernism and modernism to postmodernism, and each one held for less time than the one before.

On the feed, a look can arrive and disappear in a few weeks, because platforms push whatever is already working and everyone copies it until it's tired.

For product work, that means knowing the difference between a trend and a choice I'd make anyway, because hierarchy, spacing, contrast and readable type don't go out of date the way a gradient does.

## How to Build It

I started with no design background, so I think taste can be taught, or at least learned, by looking at a lot of work and asking why some of it works.

The first part is looking widely. [Cosmos](https://www.cosmos.so), [Are.na](https://www.are.na), [Pinterest](https://www.pinterest.com) and [X](https://x.com) are a start, but so are films, records, buildings, clothes and old hardware, because things from outside the field are where the less obvious ideas come from.

The second part is comparing: putting two things side by side, deciding which is better and saying why, because the why is the part that sticks.

Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks are full of water, birds, machines and anatomy, because he looked at everything and wrote down what he saw.

## Where It Ends Up

Over time the comparisons pile up until most choices don't need reasoning through, because they've been seen before. When people say someone has taste, I think they usually mean that person has looked at enough to know.
